# 🚀 AI Apps Are Incredibly Competitive. Why Am I Still Building Xworkmate During My Gap Year?

![Cartoon IT Guy Coding with AI in Cafe](/assets/images/cartoon_it_coffee.jpg)

After reading the leaked transcript of the conversation with Liang Wenfeng, I became even more convinced of one thing: **The path of AI cannot be judged solely by whether it makes money right now.**

## Why Keep Building AI Apps?

When many people look at the AI App space, their first reaction is: **It's too competitive.**

Chatbots, writing assistants, search engines, productivity tools, Agents—the market is flooded with identical products, funding stories, and homogenization. More realistically, the vast majority of AI Apps are not profitable right now. You might build something today, only to have it wiped out tomorrow by a new feature released by major foundational model providers.

But I still want to keep building. Not because I think the app I'm working on, **[Xworkmate](https://console.svc.plus/products/xworkmate)**, is guaranteed to go viral, but because I know this is how I train myself to enter the next generation of production.

## A Key Judgment: AI Competition Is Becoming an Ecosystem Battle

In the past, Nvidia's greatest strength wasn't just its GPUs; it was the **CUDA ecosystem**. Developers, frameworks, operators, and toolchains all revolved around it. Even if domestic chips could catch up in terms of hardware, they were often blocked by one sentence: "The ecosystem is lacking; it's unusable."

But this situation is loosening. Liang Wenfeng mentioned that although V3 training still uses Nvidia GPUs, it almost no longer relies on CUDA. They wrote an advanced compiler called TileLang themselves, and based everything else on TileLang. The hardware might temporarily remain Nvidia, but the software ecosystem doesn't necessarily have to be locked down by CUDA anymore.

This is extremely important for China's AI industry. If this approach can run successfully on Huawei's chips, the old perception that "domestic chips lack an ecosystem" could be rewritten. **The real bottleneck will shift from ecosystem issues to production capacity issues.**

It's not that China lacks R&D or design capabilities; often, the gap lies in limited resources. Huawei's 950 super-node might be able to replace GB200 and GB300 at the task level, at the cost of needing four chips to match one, plus being roughly two years behind. It sounds like a gap, but looking from another angle—abundant electricity, strong engineering capabilities, and excellent cost-compression abilities—if domestic GPU production gradually catches up, mainland China's AI advantage might not be having the strongest single chip. Instead, it will be using massive capacity and lower comprehensive costs to **turn AI into a truly accessible infrastructure.**

## Another Judgment: AGI Is a Pragmatic Staircase, Not Mysticism

AGI shouldn't be spoken of too mystically. It's not a single leap to machines possessing complete self-awareness. It's a staircase that must be climbed step by step:

1. **Chat** → Enabling AI to converse;
2. **Work** → Bringing AI into real workflows;
3. **Agent** → Having AI break down tasks, use tools, and execute objectives;
4. **Moving Forward** → Continuous learning, self-questioning, self-reflection.

The "self-reflection" here isn't self-awareness in a sci-fi sense. It's about accumulating context in specific environments, reviewing experiences, identifying problems, and proactively suggesting the next steps.

This is also the biggest difference between current AI and a real employee. When a new employee joins a company, after two months they know who Xiao Wang is, how the processes work, and what doesn't need to be explicitly stated. But with current AI, you have to re-feed it the context every single time. It's very smart, but it hasn't truly entered your environment yet. **The key to the future isn't just how well a model answers, but whether it can continuously learn and accumulate an understanding of you, the business, and the scenario through successive tasks.**

## So I Re-evaluated What I'm Doing

Building AI Apps might not make money in the short term, and they might soon be overshadowed by larger platforms—**but they are an essential entry point to understanding AI.**

Only by building products with your own hands will you know what users actually want. Only by integrating APIs, designing interactions, and handling edge cases will you touch the true boundaries of a model's capabilities. Only by putting an idea into a real-world scenario will you know whether you're just building a feature or creating a workflow that can evolve alongside the model.

For me, the gap period isn't a pause; it's accumulating experience in a different way. While others see it as an empty space, I prefer to see it as an **active opportunity to switch tracks**. AI Apps might not be the endgame, but they force me to understand products, models, users, workflows, and commercial realities. Even if it doesn't make money, the process itself is bringing me a little closer to the future.

## Finally, About Choices

I know I'm a "three-nothings" person: no dependents, no massive mortgage or car loans, and no immediate cash flow pressure to support a large family. It sounds like a disadvantage, but at this stage, it actually grants me a rare kind of freedom.

I still have room for trial and error, and I won't starve just yet, so some paths simply have to be walked. Many people see this period as undignified, unstable, and unprofitable. But once a bird returns to the free sky, no one genuinely wants to fly back to the cage. Stability is safe, of course, but some kinds of stability essentially train people slowly until they no longer believe they have other possibilities.

I don't want to choose the most error-free path during an era of the most rapid changes. The opportunities in the next phase will not belong to those who merely watch trends from the sidelines—**they belong to those who dismantle the trends with their own hands, make mistakes, recombine things, and grow new capabilities from within.**

I treat these 10 months as a real bet. I'm not betting on an App going viral; I'm betting that I refuse to retreat back into the old system.
